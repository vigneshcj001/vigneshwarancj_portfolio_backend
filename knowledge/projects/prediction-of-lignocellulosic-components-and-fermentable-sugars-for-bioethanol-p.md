# Prediction of lignocellulosic components and fermentable sugars for bioethanol production by machine learning approach

This study explores efficient bioethanol production from waste biomass using an integrated approach of nanotechnology, green pretreatment methods, and machine learning. Twenty different lignocellulosic biomass samples (mainly fruit peels and agro-wastes) were pretreated using physical, chemical, and novel physicochemical methods — including nanocatalyst-assisted ionic liquid combined with ultrasound — to extract fermentable sugars. Iron nanoparticles synthesised from Musa acuminata peels improved biomass breakdown. Machine learning models — Decision Tree, Random Forest, and LGBM — were trained on experimental data to predict biomass composition and ethanol yield. Random Forest and Decision Tree showed high accuracy in predicting lignocellulosic content and sugar yield. A BFuel web platform was developed to store and display experimental data.
